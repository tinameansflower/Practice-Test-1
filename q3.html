Although both desktop publishing and typesetting fall under the printing industry, Typesetting is a very old, traditional technique that used physical tools such as wooden letters or metal stamps to create prints such as newspapers, posters, and business cards (de Melker, 2026). Every single letter was hand-placed onto the sheet; this meant the typesetter must be very precise with what they put down, including line spacing, alignment, the layout, readability, etc (de Melker, 2026). Additionally, typesetting was incredibly time-consuming; however, as difficult and time-consuming it was, it was unique because of it (de Melker, 2026). Making it difficult to replicate because of how everything is handcrafted, made, and intentionally positioned, giving it that authentic appearance (de Melker, 2026).

Though there has been a rise in digital printing. Printing has changed completely from the time spent to the content prints contain. Desktop publishing allows text, images, and designs to be done and edited quickly, whereas typesetting must be done manually (de Melker, 2026). Desktop publishing allows prints to be mass-produced, though typesetting may be possible; however, it is incredibly time-consuming. Desktop publishing also allows ease of editing, adjustments, various styles, and the quality will always be consistent (de Melker, 2026). Both have high precision of alignment; however, typesetting relies on typesetters to be more attentive and accurate, as making adjustments and fixing mistakes after they are printed causes an entire redo or a need to adapt to the mistake. There are limited physical materials, while digitally there's no limit (de Melker, 2026). Allowing accessibility for all users with or without experience, to learn and participate.
