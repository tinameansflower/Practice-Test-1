Emphasis and strengthened text

Tags are enclosed in angle brackets; don't skip heading levels. Gradually increase the number until you're satisfied. For emphasizing and strengthening text, you use the same angle brackets; however, fill the middle with "em" for a bolder, more prominent element, emphasized through italics. for example, Hi Tina was here. The text was italicized.

As for bolding, instead of "em" it would be "strong". By replacing the term "em" with "strong" between the angle brackets, the element will be emphasized through bolding. As shown. Hi Tina was here.

Here are the two side by side.

Em: Hi Tina was here,

Strong: Hi Tina was here.

the layout should go along these lines:<"em/strong"> text insert <"em/strong">

Explaination

Why em & strong are better than i & b

The em and strong stand for what they mean. For instance, em stands for emphasis, telling the user that if you use "em," you are emphasizing a section of text. The same thing for strong: it is meant to indicate strong importance, telling the user that the following text holds importance. While visually, both the "i" and "b" work, the reason "em" or "strong" is preferred is due to the context and meaning behind it. Typically, for accessibility users, the visually impaired, em and strong are meant to describe the surrounding text; it's not merely shown for style. In short, "i/b" isn't preferred due to the lack of accessibility for the disabled. "em/strong" fixes that gap, allowing context and meaning for the visually impaired to understand that a sentence or word is emphasized or holds strong importance.
